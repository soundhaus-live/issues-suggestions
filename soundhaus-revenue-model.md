# Soundhaus: Revenue Model Analysis & Monetization Strategy
### Co-Founder Briefing Document — May 2026

---

## Executive Summary

The current pay-per-event model ($20/event, no membership, no audience revenue, no venue share) is significantly underperforming relative to the community asset already built. STL has 300 musicians on its roster. On any given event night roughly 88% of that roster is not playing — they are in the community, receiving value, and paying nothing. The per-event model only captures revenue from the minority who show up each night.

The path forward is a membership model built on **access and identity perks, not discounts** — layered with per-event fees for all players, venue bar revenue share, and a community groups platform tier. This document covers the full model, the math at every stage, and the path to $1M in revenue.

## The Problem with the Current Model

**Current state:**
- $20/event per musician
- First 2 events free (trial)
- No membership, no audience revenue, no venue share
- Typical event: 45 musicians (STL average)
- Horn nights: 55 musicians (STL average)

**Regular night gross:** 45 avg x $20 = $900/event
**Horn night gross:** 55 x $20 = $1,100/event
**1.5 events/month STL gross:** ~$1,400/month blended (15 regular + 3 horn annually = $16,800/year)

That is real money but it has three structural problems:

**Problem 1 — The 88% problem.**
STL has 300 musicians. A typical event has 35 players. That means 265 musicians (88%) are in the community on any given night and paying nothing. Membership captures value from the whole roster, not just who shows up tonight.

**Problem 2 — The $20 friction.**
Asking a musician to pay before experiencing the product is hard. The free trial solves acquisition but creates a trust-then-charge dynamic. Membership reframes the conversation from "pay to play" to "join a community."

**Problem 3 — No other revenue layers.**
No audience revenue despite running a genuine show every month. No venue share despite filling their dead Wednesday. Both are addressable now.

---

## The Core Revenue Architecture

```
LAYER 1:  Musician Membership           ← Recurring, captures whole roster
LAYER 2:  Per-Event Fees (Haus 1) (all players)  ← Members pay less; casuals pay full price
LAYER 3:  Venue Bar Revenue Share       ← Performance-based, aligns venue incentives
LAYER 4:  Community Groups        ← Platform SaaS tier, nearly pure margin
FUTURE:   Guest Cover Charge            ← Audience revenue, introduce once model is proven
```

## Membership Tiers

### Philosophy: Perks Over Discounts

The membership value should not live primarily in the per-event discount. Platforms that lead with discounts cap their value proposition at math. Platforms that lead with access, identity, and capability create emotional loyalty with no ceiling.

Discord Nitro, Twitch subscriptions, Peloton — none lead with discounts. They lead with what you can do and who you are in the community. Soundhaus membership should work the same way.

The $5 event discount is the floor — it makes the math tangibly better. The perks are what make membership feel essential.

### Signup Window — Why This Matters Most

Early signup access is the single most compelling perk for a musician who cares about what they play. The best parts go first. Being 24 hours ahead of the general pool — or 48 hours ahead as a premium — means consistently getting the songs and parts you want rather than picking through what's left.

This is worth $10/month to any musician who has shown up to an event and found their preferred part already taken.

---
### Increased Minimum Songs per session (Premium) 
The default minimum songs per session is set to 3 to maximize the number of musicians that can fill the roster. Premium members will bump that up to 4 songs per session (if they choose 4 or more) allowing them to perform additional songs from the roster, thereby making their money "go further"


---
### Pre Registration (Premium)

Pre-registration will allow a premium member to fill out their registration before the registration window opens and submit it right at opening. They will not see any part lock-out during their registration and their parts will get submitted in the order they filled it out. This allows premium members a much earlier window to select their parts.

---
### Guaranteed Parts System (Standard and Premium)
Standard members receive 1 guaranteed part claim per year and Premium members receive 4 guaranteed part claims per year. A claimed part is locked to that musician and removed from the general signup pool.

**How tie-breakers work when two premiums want the same part:**
- First-claim wins (first to submit in the app)
- Simultaneous claims (same minute): membership tenure breaks the tie — longer-standing premium wins
- Admin override available in edge cases

In practice this is rare. A 35-musician event across multiple songs has dozens of parts available. Two premiums competing for the exact same part on the exact same song is an occasional exception, not a regular occurrence.

---

### Milestone Badge System

Badges create goals. Goals drive attendance. Attendance builds community. Every badge is a reason to come back.

**Performance milestones:**
| Badge | Trigger |

| 🎸 First Show | Plays first Soundhaus event |
| 🎸🎸 5 Shows | — |
| ⭐ 10 Shows | "Regulars" tier |
| ⭐⭐ 25 Shows | "Core" tier |
| 🏆 50 Shows | "Veteran" tier |
| 💎 100 Shows | "Soundhaus Legend" |

**Event type badges:**
| Badge | Trigger |
|---|---|
| 🎺 Horn Night Veteran | 3+ horn section events |
| 🌆 Multi-City | Played in 2+ Soundhaus cities |
| 🏙️ City Pioneer | Among first 50 musicians in a new city |

**Community badges:**
| Badge | Trigger |
|---|---|
| 🎵 Song Suggester | Had a song accepted into the library |
| ⭐ Top Rated | Peer ratings above threshold across 5+ events |
| 🔁 Streak | 3 consecutive events, 6 consecutive events |

**Key rule:** Milestone badges stay on your profile forever regardless of membership status. A musician who lets their membership lapse keeps their "50 Shows" badge. Their history is theirs. This removes the fear of losing things if you pause, which lowers the barrier to initial signup.

---

### Loyalty Event Credit

Play consistently, get rewarded tangibly.

- **Standard:** Free event credit ($20 value) after every 10 events
- **Premium:** Free event credit ($20 value) after every 8 events

At 18 events/year:
- Standard earns 1 credit/year ($20 tangible value)
- Premium earns 2 credits/year ($40 tangible value)

This creates a visible goal in the app ("3 events until your next free night") and rewards exactly the musicians you most want to retain — your most consistent players.

**Old pitch (discount model):** *"Play 8 events and your membership pays for itself."*

**New pitch (perks model):** *"Be first in the room. Play more songs. Own your part. Build your legacy."*

## Break-Even Analysis

With a $5 discount ($20 member vs $25 non-member), the pure discount break-even at $10/month is:

$120/year ÷ $5 savings/event = **24 events to break even on discount alone**

At 18 events/year, the discount saves $90 against a $120 membership cost — a net cost of $30/year after discount. This confirms the discount cannot carry the membership alone, which is why the perks framework exists. The $5 discount softens the cost; the perks justify it.

For premium at $20/month: $240/year, same $5 discount, $90 in savings — net cost $150/year. The perks (4 songs, 6 guaranteed parts, gold border, 48hr early access, multi-city at member rate) need to justify that $150 for the right musician. They do.

## Admin Compensation Model

The admin role is a **paid community manager and event host position** — not a platform partner. The economics must reflect this.

**What Soundhaus brings:** The entire product, the brand, the song library, the musician reputation data, the customer relationships, the multi-city value. If an admin left tomorrow, the musicians, the platform, and the community would all stay.

**What the admin brings:** Local presence, event night execution, light recruiting, community cheerleading.

**Structure: per-event flat fee, not a monthly retainer or revenue percentage.**

A per-event model aligns admin compensation directly with activity — the admin is paid for the events they run, not for months where activity is light. It also scales correctly as new cities ramp up from 1 event/month to 1.5, without requiring a separate tier renegotiation.

| City Stage | Events/Month | Admin Per Event | Admin Monthly |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early / not yet viable | 0–1 | $0 — founding role | $0 |
| Getting traction | 1 | $350 | $350 |
| Growing | 1–1.5 | $350 | $350–525 |
| Mature | 1.5 | $350 | $525 |

**Non-cash compensation that matters to the right admin:**
- Free annual Premium membership ($240 value)
- "City Lead" title on Soundhaus profile — visible across all cities
- First consideration for private event and corporate booking revenue in their city

**The right admin is someone who loves their local music community and wants a meaningful part-time role in it.** Anyone pushing hard for equity or a high revenue percentage is the wrong person for the role.

## Community Groups — Haus Community

A second-tier product running on the same infrastructure as Haus 1, but self-organized and not curated by Soundhaus. Think of it as a premium fitness studio renting floor space to independent instructors who bring their own members.

### Who Creates a Community Group

- A church praise band coordinating weekly songs and sharing charts
- A classic rock cover group meeting monthly
- A School of Rock alumni group running informal gig nights
- A corporate team with quarterly jam sessions
- A college music department running student ensemble nights
- A neighborhood bar running its own informal musician night without a Soundhaus admin

These groups will never be Haus 1 — they either can't meet the vetting bar, don't want the structure, or are running something fundamentally different. But they have a real need for exactly the tools Soundhaus has already built.

### Feature Comparison

| Feature | Haus 1 | Haus Community |
|---|---|---|
| Song library access | Full | Full |
| Setlist tools | Full | Full |
| Message boards per song | Full | Full |
| Part assignment tools | Admin-managed | Self-managed by organizer |
| Event creation tools | Full | Full |
| Member management | Full | Full |
| Soundhaus vetting | Yes | No — organizer decides |
| Soundhaus admin runs event | Yes | No — organizer runs it |
| Soundhaus brand | Full | "Powered by Soundhaus" only |
| Access to Haus 1 events | Included in membership | Separate Haus 1 membership required |

### Organizer Subscription Tiers

| Tier | Price | Group Size |
|---|---|---|
| **Starter** | $29/mo or $249/yr | Up to 25 members |
| **Standard** | $49/mo or $399/yr | Up to 100 members |
| **Multi-Group** | $89/mo or $699/yr | Unlimited members, up to 5 groups |

Meetup charges organizers $45/month. Soundhaus offers significantly more value (full coordination platform, song library, part assignment, message boards) so pricing at or above Meetup is defensible.

### Per-Event Platform Cut

Organizers can charge their members through the platform. Soundhaus retains 15% of all fees collected.

- Organizer sets their own price ($5, $10, $20 — their choice)
- Example: 20 musicians at $15/event = $300 collected, $45 to Soundhaus, $255 to organizer
- No admin cost, no venue coordination, no event management — pure margin

### Strategic Value Beyond Revenue

**Feeder into Haus 1.** Community members who love the platform are the warmest possible leads for Haus 1 upgrades. They already know the tools and trust the song library. The upgrade pitch is easy.

**Geographic intelligence.** A thriving Community Group in Nashville tells you there's demand there before you commit to a Haus 1 launch. Community Groups are demand validation at zero operational cost.

**Platform lock-in.** An organizer with 2 years of song library history, 80 members, and message board content for 60 songs is not migrating to a competitor. Switching costs are structural.

**Brand protection.** The "Powered by Soundhaus" label on community events clearly separates self-organized groups from the curated Haus 1 experience. Haus 1 quality is protected.

### Naming Options

- **Haus Sessions** — organizer-led, informal, the grassroots tier
- **Haus Open** — signals accessibility without premium association
- **Soundhaus Groups** — clean, descriptive, Meetup-adjacent language
- **Haus Community** — straightforward, maps to the Meetup mental model

With 150 annual members per city + 50 community groups nationally:

| Revenue Source | Annual |
|---|---|
| 30 mature Haus 1 cities x $33,720 net | $1,011,600 |
| 50 community groups | $33,996 |
| **Combined** | **$1,045,596** |

**30 cities is the realistic target** with 150 annual members per city and community groups running nationally.

### What Has to Be True

| Variable | Required | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Annual members per city | 150 | 50% conversion of a 300-musician roster |
| Event size | 45 regular / 55 horn | Already the STL average |
| Member attendance rate | 60% | Conservative; grows as community matures |

The 150-member conversion is the most important variable. STL is already at 300 roster musicians — converting half to annual membership at $10/month is a realistic near-term goal, not an aspirational one.

### Why Raising the Membership Price Is Not the Answer

The temptation is to raise the standard membership to $15/month to improve per-city economics. Here is why that backfires:

At $15/month ($180/year) with a $5 event discount ($5 savings/event):
- Break-even: $180 ÷ $5 = **36 events** — never achievable
- At 18 events/year: saves $90, paid $180 — net cost $90/year with no math upside

The value proposition collapses. The path to $1M is more members per city, not a higher price that makes the membership harder to sell.

## Future Revenue Layers

Do not pursue these until the core model is running cleanly in at least two cities.

### Guest Cover Charge

With 30–35 musicians performing a full-band set over a full evening, Soundhaus runs a real show. A $10–12 cover for non-playing guests on regular nights, $15 on horn nights, is a natural next revenue layer.

- Conservative estimate: 20–30 guests per event
- $500–750/month per mature city at near-zero incremental cost
- Horn nights with 50 musicians are the strongest argument — that is a legitimate ticketed show
- Introduce once memberships are established and the audience habit is built
- At 37 mature cities with guest cover: adds $220,000–330,000/year to the model

### Music Gear Sponsorships

300 vetted, active, instrument-playing musicians in a single city is an extremely targeted audience for Guitar Center, Fender, D'Addario, Sweetwater, or local music shops.

- City sponsorship: $500–1,000/month per city
- Logo on event materials, email mentions, table presence at events
- Pitchable in STL now; other cities at 100+ members

### Corporate and Private Events

The Soundhaus format — learn your assigned part independently, show up, perform with strangers — is a genuinely novel corporate team-building experience.

- Price point: $2,000–5,000 per private event
- The format, tools, venue relationships, and admin experience already exist
- Keep branding separate from the community product
- Test with one pilot private event in STL

### Musician Services Marketplace

Once reliability data is deep and the social graph is dense, power a referral marketplace:
- Venues and event planners need vetted musicians for private events
- We know who shows up, who prepares, and who delivers
- This is a premium matchmaking asset no competitor can replicate
- Timeline: 18–24 months out

## Immediate Action Plan

The membership revenue model cannot launch until the platform supports it. All membership-related monetization — tiers, perks, badges, event discounts, guaranteed parts, early signup windows — is blocked on product development. The action plan is sequenced accordingly.

---

### Phase 1 — Platform Build (Prerequisite for Everything)

Nothing in Phase 2 or 3 moves forward until these are complete.

- [ ] **User funnel architecture** — Define and build the full user journey from unknown visitor → free account → community member → paid Standard → paid Premium. Every state transition needs a clear trigger, UI flow, and data model.
- [ ] **Payment processing integration** — Integrate stripe payment processor. Must support monthly/annual subscriptions, per-event fees, and one-time charges. Needs to handle failed payments, cancellations, and refunds gracefully.
- [ ] **Membership tier system** — Build Standard and Premium tiers into the app with all associated access rules: event fee rates, signup window timing, song slot limits, guaranteed part claim tracking.
- [ ] **Badge and milestone engine** — Build the system that tracks event attendance, issues milestone badges (First Show, 5 Shows, 10 Shows, etc.), and displays them on musician profiles. Badges must persist regardless of membership status.
- [ ] **Profile upgrade layer** — Build the portrait border system (silver for Standard, gold/animated for Premium), membership badge display, instrument specialization tags, and City Lead designation.
- [ ] **Early signup window enforcement** — Build the logic that opens registration 48hrs early for Premium, 24hrs early for Standard, and at general open for non-members. Must handle edge cases (member lapses between event creation and signup window).
- [ ] **Loyalty credit tracker** — Build the counter that tracks events attended per member, issues a free event credit at the 10-event (Standard) and 8-event (Premium) thresholds, and applies it at checkout.
- [ ] **Guaranteed parts claim system** — Build the mechanism for Standard (1/year) and Premium (4/year) guaranteed part claims, including the tie-breaker logic and admin override capability.
- [ ] **Full Song Library and User Management Operations** — Build the operational web app for user tracking, communication channels (email,facebook,phone,tech support) along with sales funnel and list management workflows (vetting and approval process). Add AI automation to streamline and improve city build out process.

---

### Phase 2 — Soft Launch (Once Platform Build is Complete)

- [ ] **STL membership beta** — Invite existing STL roster to be founding members at a discounted rate or possibly free for specific users. Validate payment flow, badge issuance, and signup window behavior with a known audience before opening broadly.
- [ ] **KC founding member offer** — Email KC attendees from both events with the founding member pitch. These are the warmest leads; do not wait longer than the platform build allows.
- [ ] **Validate the funnel** — Measure conversion at each stage: unknown → free account → paid member. Identify where drop-off happens and fix it before scaling to new cities.
- [ ] **Admin per-event payment flow** — Build or confirm the mechanism for paying the city admin $350/event. Must be reliable before additional cities hit their first paid session.

---

### Phase 3 — Operational (Run in Parallel with Phase 1)

These do not depend on the membership build and can move now.

- [ ] **Open bar revenue share conversations** with Tin Roof KC and The Duck Room STL. Frame it as partnership — we fill your dead Wednesday/Thursday, help us sustain the operation.
- [ ] **Formalize venue partnerships** in all 4 cities. Written agreement with a recurring slot.
- [ ] **Begin musician reliability logging** — attendance, preparation quality, peer ratings.
- [ ] **Build learning resources for top 30 catalog songs** — chord charts, reference recording links, message board starters. This improves event quality regardless of membership status.
- [ ] **Define Chicago launch playbook** —  founding member offer structure, launch event timeline. Have the plan ready before the platform build is done so Chicago can move fast once it is.
- [ ] **Press push in each active city** — local alt-weeklies, music blogs, local TV. The "full band, no rehearsal, strangers" angle is a strong story hook. Earned media costs nothing and builds the roster that feeds membership conversion.
- [ ] **Monitor Rockin'1000 Jam expansion** — quarterly check on their US schedule. If they announce Chicago, accelerate the launch timeline.

---

### Phase 4 — Community Groups (After Membership is Stable)

Do not scope or build the Haus Community product until the Haus 1 membership system is proven and stable. Community Groups run on the same infrastructure — building them before that infrastructure is solid creates compounding technical debt.

- [ ] Scope the Community Groups organizer subscription flow
- [ ] Define what platform features are available to organizers vs. reserved for Haus 1
- [ ] Build organizer-facing event creation, member management, and per-event payment collection with the 15% platform cut

## The Bottom Line

The revenue model needs two things: a shift from pure per-event fees to annual memberships that capture the full roster value, and a clear perks framework that makes membership feel worth having rather than a discount card.

The math to $1M requires roughly 30 cities at 150 annual members each — achievable at a pace of 2 new cities per quarter over 3–4 years with the community groups layer running nationally alongside.

The membership pitch is not "save money." It is: **be first in the room, play more songs, own your part, and build your legacy.** That is a product people stay subscribed to through the months they miss an event, because they are invested in the community, not just optimizing for transaction cost.
